LAMP

Long-range Adaptive Memory Propagation

A lamp that carries information far across space and time — a differential-equation GNN for dynamic graphs that learns exactly how much to remember and how much to forget.

De Castelli · Gravina · Eliasof · Schönlieb · Bacciu

The one idea

Conservation vs. forgetting

Most graph models leak: signals from distant nodes or distant moments fade before they arrive. LAMP keeps a near-lossless channel open — then learns a per-dimension dimmer (the term Γ) that turns dissipation up only where the signal is noise.

Toy #1

The dimmer

Γ is the dissipation knob. Fire a pulse, then watch how long the light survives. Turn it down and the past persists (long-range memory); turn it up and only recent signal remains (noise filtering).

Toy #2 — the benchmark itself

Colored leaf counting

Our long-range stress test. Build a spider tree, then watch its leaves blink colored signals over time. Each leaf blinks only sometimes. The root lamp must count how many blinks matched its own colour, propagated all the way up from the deepest leaves — but it won't tell you. Count along, then guess.

Toy #3

The lighthouse

Same physics, spatial view. A beam sweeps a field of nodes. Without dissipation the afterglow trail reaches the far edge — distant nodes still hear the signal. Crank Γ and the beam dies at the horizon, like an ordinary GNN over-squashing.

Γ damping 0.10

Does it work

Yes — by a wide margin
